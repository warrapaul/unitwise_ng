Unitwise: Production-readiness review of Chat, maintenance requests and follow-ups

Your current UI already has a good foundation: a conversation list, a message panel, tenant-to-landlord conversations, building channels, broadcast functionality, tenant and admin interfaces, and a rent detail modal with charges, adjustments, waivers, and payment recording.

The biggest opportunity is to turn these into a proper property-management communication system that supports multiple buildings, multiple administrators, tenant lifecycle controls, real-time delivery, maintenance workflows, and auditable financial changes.

3. Multi-building agency and administrator model
Your point about an agency having several buildings and several administrators is fundamental. Design around the agency as the organization, buildings as managed properties, and users as people with scoped permissions.



Treat this table as a suggested default, not a hard-coded rule. Each agency should be able to assign permissions based on its operations.
Sender identity
Every message should store the actual sender's user ID, not just a text label.

Display identity in a format such as:
- Grace W. · Landlord
- John K. · Caretaker
- Mary N. · Agent
For group messages, display the sender name or configured abbreviation beside the message. In a direct conversation, show the other person's name and role in the header.

If staff send an official broadcast on behalf of the agency, show both the agency identity and the responsible staff member where appropriate.

Never share staff accounts. Individual accounts preserve accountability, auditability, and security when a staff member leaves the agency.
Group configuration
Provide a group settings screen with:
- Group name and description. (DEFAULT TO BUILDING NAME)
- Associated agency and building.
- Posting permission: admins only or all eligible members.
- Who can add and remove members.
- Whether new members can see previous messages.
- Whether members can reply to announcements.
For safety, membership should be controlled by the platform's tenancy records and staff assignments, not by trusting a group name or a client-side selection.
4. Tenant activation and building group visibility
This is a critical access-control requirement.

A newly created tenant record should not automatically grant access to private building communications. Access should depend on an activated tenant account, an eligible tenancy, the tenancy dates, the building, and any applicable verification requirements.

Recommended access rules:
- Invited but not activated: Can complete onboarding, but cannot access tenant group conversations.
- Confirmed, eligible tenancy: Can access the appropriate building groups and receive future broadcasts.
- Moved out or tenancy terminated: Lose access to current building groups according to the configured access policy.
- Staff member removed from a building: Lose access to that building's private communications 

For existing group messages, choose one explicit policy:
1. New members see messages from the time they join.
2. New members can access group history.
3. New members see a limited period of history.
I would default to messages from the time of joining, with historical access controlled separately for groups that need it.

The backend must enforce these rules for message retrieval, attachments, search, WebSocket subscriptions, and notifications. Hiding a conversation in the UI is not sufficient.

7. How maintenance should connect to rent adjustments
Your existing rent modal already has a useful foundation for charges, waivers, adjustments, and payment recording. The missing piece is a controlled workflow linking an approved maintenance expense to a tenant's financial ledger.

The most important design rule is: a maintenance request must not automatically change rent or create a payable tenant charge.

A repair may be the landlord's responsibility, a tenant's responsibility, a shared cost, or a cost that cannot be charged to the tenant at all. That decision needs an authorized approval process based on the tenancy agreement, applicable law, and the agency's policies.
Recommended end-to-end workflow





Example using your existing rent modal
Suppose Room 101 has the following monthly statement:
Item	Amount (KES)
Rent	6,000
Garbage	200
Water	0
Total before maintenance	6,200

A plumbing repair costs KES 1,500.

The system should first record that as a maintenance expense and determine whether it is billable to the tenant. If an authorized person approves the tenant charge, the ledger can then display:
Item	Amount (KES)
Rent	6,000
Garbage	200
Water	0
Approved plumbing charge	1,500
Total due before payments	7,700

This is an illustration, not a recommendation that this particular repair should be charged to the tenant.

The maintenance charge should include a link back to the maintenance request, a reason, evidence or supporting documentation where applicable, the approver, the approval timestamp, and the applicable billing period.
Keep three financial concepts separate
- Property expense: The amount the landlord or agency incurs for repair work.
- Tenant charge: An amount that has been reviewed and authorized for billing to the tenant.
- Rent adjustment: A change to the actual rent obligation, such as an approved credit or rent reduction.
Do not use one generic adjustment field for all three.

I would also add the following to the existing rent modal:
- Source reference, e.g. MT-2026-0042.
- Charge category and explanation.
- Estimated versus actual cost.
- Approval status and approver.
- Reversal or credit functionality.
- Supporting receipt or invoice.
- Ledger history showing the original entry and all subsequent changes.
Prefer append-only financial transactions and reversing entries over silently editing an already posted charge. That makes reconciliation and audit reporting much more reliable.
8. WhatsApp integration: recommended approach
WhatsApp should be an additional communication channel connected to Unitwise conversations, not a replacement for the platform's internal messaging.

Use the official WhatsApp Business Platform rather than an unofficial WhatsApp Web automation library. Official documentation is available through Meta's WhatsApp documentation.
What the integration should support
Inbound WhatsApp messages

A tenant sends a WhatsApp message to the agency's registered business number. Unitwise identifies the sender where possible, finds the relevant conversation, and routes the message to the appropriate agency or support inbox.

If the tenant cannot be confidently matched, keep the message in an unassigned inbox rather than exposing another tenant's information.


Outbound WhatsApp messages

Authorized staff can respond from Unitwise, and the message is sent through the official API. Record the message, sender, recipient, channel, delivery state, and provider message ID.


Unified conversation history

Display internal and WhatsApp messages together when they belong to the same conversation. Label the source of each message and prevent duplicated webhook events from creating duplicate messages.


Notifications and automation

Send approved notifications for new messages, maintenance updates, scheduled visits, and selected announcements. Allow tenants to choose supported communication preferences.



A key architectural decision: internal messages versus WhatsApp
Keep a single canonical message record with a separate delivery record for each external channel.

For example, a staff member may send a maintenance update internally and also send it via WhatsApp to the tenant. The application should know whether those are two separate communications or the same message sent across multiple channels.

A practical data structure would include:
- conversation_id
- message_id
- sender_user_id
- channel (internal, whatsapp, and future channels)
- delivery_status
- sent_at, delivered_at, and read_at where available
- provider_error, if delivery failed
Do not assume that a message sent through WhatsApp has been delivered or read. Update the UI only when the provider's delivery-status webhook confirms the corresponding state.

For production, treat WhatsApp as a channel for individually addressed messages and approved business notifications unless the specific group capability you need is officially available and suitable for your account.

For example, a building-wide announcement can be represented as one Unitwise broadcast with multiple individual WhatsApp deliveries. The tenant should see the same announcement inside Unitwise and, if eligible and opted in, receive it on WhatsApp.
Important WhatsApp rules
- Obtain the necessary consent for WhatsApp communications.
- Support opt-out and communication preferences.
- Observe applicable template and customer-service messaging rules.
- Secure access tokens and webhook verification.
- Apply rate limits, retry policies, and idempotency.
- Store enough information to audit outbound messages and delivery failures.
- Avoid sending sensitive tenancy or financial details in a notification preview.
WhatsApp should be an integration behind the same authorization and messaging services as internal chat, not an independent application with a separate set of tenant permissions.


9. Real-time messaging and notification architecture
For production, real-time delivery should be designed as a server-side messaging service with persistent storage and a separate notification pipeline.

A WebSocket connection alone does not make messaging reliable. Messages must still be saved, authorized, delivered to offline users later, and protected against duplicate sends.
Recommended message flow





Suggested technology stack
If you are building Unitwise as a modern web application, this is a sensible production architecture. The exact choices should fit your existing backend and deployment.
Layer	Recommendation	Purpose
Primary database		Conversations, messages, membership, tickets and financial records
Real-time transport	WebSockets, e.g. Socket.IO or native WebSockets	Live delivery and typing indicators
Event distribution	Redis Pub/Sub or a durable message broker	Distributing events across application instances
Background jobs	Queue workers with retries	Notifications, WhatsApp delivery, attachments and scheduled reminders
File storage	 object storage	Images, videos, invoices and repair evidence
Web push	Web Push or supported browser push infrastructure	Notifications when users are away from the app
Mobile push	Firebase Cloud Messaging and/or APNs	Notifications to supported mobile apps
External messaging	WhatsApp Business Platform	Business messaging and delivery status
Monitoring	Structured logs, error tracking and metrics	Diagnosing failures and delivery delays

For a smaller initial deployment, you may not need every infrastructure component immediately. Start with DB, a reliable real-time layer, background jobs, and proper persistence; introduce a dedicated broker or more specialized infrastructure as load and operational requirements justify it.
Essential real-time behaviours
Your acceptance criteria should include:
- A new message appears on the recipient's screen without manually refreshing.
- Messages are stored before they are acknowledged as successfully sent.
- Offline users receive the message when they reconnect.
- Reconnection retrieves messages missed during disconnection.
- Messages retain their ordering within a conversation.
- Duplicate requests or webhook retries do not create duplicate messages.
- Multiple browser tabs or devices do not cause inconsistent read states.
- Unread counts update in real time.
- A failed external notification does not delete or invalidate the internal message.
- If the sender's request times out after the message was saved, retrying does not send a second copy.

For every notification, distinguish between:
- In-app notification.
- Push notification.
- Email.
- WhatsApp.
- SMS, if enabled for appropriate use cases.

A notification should contain a safe preview, a link to the relevant record, and enough context for the user to identify what needs attention. The linked page must perform a fresh authorization check.
Notification centre improvements
Your existing sidebar has notification badges. Expand this into a notification centre with:
- Read/unread states.
- Mark as read and mark all as read.
- Filter by type.
- Deep links to the relevant chat, maintenance request, or ledger entry.
- Per-event notification preferences.
- Notification delivery history for administrators where appropriate.
Avoid treating notification counts as a source of truth for messages. Unread message counts should be derived from the user's conversation read state, while notification read state should be tracked separately.

12. Recommended database model
For a multi-agency platform, I would avoid separate message tables for direct chat, group chat, broadcasts, maintenance conversations and shop conversations. A shared messaging model will be easier to extend.

A practical conceptual model is:




Core entities and important fields
Entity	Key fields and purpose
conversations	Agency, optional building, type, title, posting policy, creator, status
conversation_members	User, membership role, join/leave dates, last-read position, notification preferences
messages	Conversation, sender, content, type, timestamps, reply reference, edit/delete state
message_deliveries	Message, channel, recipient, provider reference, delivery/read status
message_attachments	Message, storage key, MIME type, file size, security scan status
maintenance_requests	Tenant, tenancy, building, room, category, priority, description, status
maintenance_assignments	Request, staff/contractor, assignment period, assigned by
work_orders	Request, scheduled visit, estimated/actual costs, completion information
maintenance_charges	Work order/request, proposed amount, billable party, approval and source
tenant_ledger_entries	Tenancy, period, transaction type, amount, reference, posting/reversal status

audit_logs	Actor, action, target, timestamp, relevant change metadata


Two especially important decisions:
1. Every conversation must have an explicit organization scope, with server-side authorization enforced independently of the UI.
2. Financial entries must be traceable back to the approved maintenance action and must not be created by directly editing a displayed balance.

15. My final recommendations
If I were prioritizing the Unitwise release, I would focus on these decisions first:
Priority	Recommendation	Why it matters
Critical	Server-side conversation authorization	Prevents privacy breaches between tenants, buildings and agencies
Critical	Persistent, real-time messaging with reconnection	Makes chat dependable rather than just visually interactive
Critical	Tenant activation and membership lifecycle	Prevents unauthorized access to building communications
Critical	Maintenance requests as structured records	Enables proper assignment, scheduling, tracking and resolution
Critical	Approved financial workflow for maintenance charges	Prevents incorrect rent balances and unauthorized charges
High	Unified conversation list and sender identity	Improves clarity for tenants, caretakers, agents and landlords
High	Event-driven notifications	Reduces missed messages, repairs and announcements
High	Delivery monitoring, retries and audit logs	Makes failures diagnosable
High	WhatsApp integration through the official API	Extends communication without creating a separate system

The single most important architectural decision is to separate messaging from business workflows while linking them together.

Chat should manage conversations and messages. Maintenance should manage requests, work orders, assignments and resolution. The financial module should manage expenses, authorized charges, adjustments and payments. Notifications should coordinate updates across all three, and WhatsApp should act as another delivery channel.

That design will give Unitwise a much stronger foundation for a multi-building, multi-administrator property-management platform, while leaving room for ecommerce support conversations and other future communication channels.
