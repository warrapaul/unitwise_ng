2.2 The most important structural correction: tenant versus tenancy
A person and their tenancy account are not the same entity.

For example, one tenant might rent Room 101 this year and Room 204 next year. The same person could also rent two rooms simultaneously, or have tenancies managed by different agencies.

The system should therefore separate:
- Person: The underlying individual or organization. (USER ACCOUNT IS NOT ACCESSIBLE TO LANDLORDS, AGENCY ADMINS ONLY VIEW TENANT ACCOUNTS OR SHARED TENANCY PROFILES)
- Renter profile: Identity details, contact information, documents, and information the renter chooses to share. (OR DEFINED BY THE SYSTEM)

- Lease: The contractual agreement, its terms, dates, signatures, renewals, and termination.
- Financial account or ledger: Charges, receipts, deposits, credits, refunds, and balances.
This distinction prevents the tenant list from becoming inaccurate when a lease expires, a tenant transfers rooms, or the same tenant returns to the property.
2.3 Improve the search and filtering model

The current filters are a good starting point, but production operations need filters that reflect the onboarding lifecycle.

Recommended filter groups:
- Onboarding: Draft, invitation sent, awaiting acceptance, profile sharing requested, details received, verification pending, verified.
- Tenancy: Pending, reserved, lease pending, scheduled to move in, active, notice given, ended, cancelled.
- Room: Building, floor, room, occupancy state.
- Financial: Up to date, partial payment, overdue, deposit outstanding.
- Identity: Verified, unverified, rejected
Add pagination, sortable columns, export permissions, and clear filters that do not unexpectedly reset unrelated search criteria.

6. Rent, security deposits, and payments received
The manual creation form and verification page both include monthly rent, security deposit, rent paid now, deposit received, payment method, date, and reference.

These are useful fields, but the current arrangement presents the greatest financial-integrity risk in the workflow.
6.1 Separate agreed amounts from actual receipts
The application must distinguish at least four concepts:
Concept	Meaning	Example
Agreed rent	Contractual monthly rent	KES 6,000
Rent charge	Amount billed for a defined period	October rent: KES 6,000
Payment received	Actual money received	KES 6,000 via M-Pesa
Outstanding balance	Charges minus valid payments and adjustments	KES 0

Apply the same principle to deposits:
- Agreed deposit.
- Deposit amount due.
- Deposit actually received.
- Deposit held.
- Deposit refunded.
- Deposit applied to permitted charges.
- Outstanding deposit balance.
Do not treat an amount entered during tenant creation as proof of payment.

The user should explicitly confirm that money was received before the system creates a receipt or payment transaction. 
6.2 Improve the initial payment interface
For rent paid now, include:
- Amount received.
- Payment date and time, where necessary.
- Billing period covered.
- Payment method.
- Transaction or receipt reference.
- Payer and recipient, where relevant.
- Recording user.
- Notes or supporting evidence, where appropriate.
For a deposit, include the same essential information but link the receipt to the deposit ledger rather than to rent.

The current M-Pesa default is sensible for a Kenyan deployment, but the application should support configurable payment methods, such as bank transfer, cash, mobile money, and other locally supported methods.

For electronic payments, prefer reconciliation against provider-confirmed transactions rather than relying exclusively on manual references. (OR ADD A TODOLATER)
6.3 Add duplicate transaction protection
A payment reference may be entered twice, or a landlord may click Save twice because the interface appears unresponsive.

Implement idempotency and duplicate detection so repeated submissions do not create multiple receipts or apply the same payment more than once.

Where an integration supplies a unique transaction ID, enforce uniqueness in the appropriate financial scope. Where a manual payment lacks a unique provider ID, use a duplicate-warning workflow rather than assuming that two identical amounts must represent the same transaction.
6.4 Use immutable financial records
Once a payment has been posted, do not silently overwrite it.

If a landlord makes a mistake, the system should support a correction, reversal, or refund transaction with:
- The original transaction reference.
- The correction reason.
- The user making the correction.
- The timestamp.
- The resulting balance.
Financial records should be auditable and linked to the appropriate tenancy and accounting period.
6.5 Deposits need a lifecycle of their own
A security deposit is not simply another payment. It may be held for a long time, refunded at the end of the tenancy, or subject to permitted deductions.

Add a deposit management record with the following:
- Amount agreed and received.
- Date received.
- Payment method and reference.
- Current deposit balance.
- Any deductions and their documented reasons.
- Refund amount and date.
- Refund transaction reference.
- Outstanding deposit balance, where applicable.
Deposit handling and permissible deductions must follow the applicable tenancy and financial rules for the deployment jurisdiction.

8. Critical authorization, privacy, and security requirements

8.1 Enforce agency-level access on the server
Every tenant, tenancy, document, payment, and lease request should be checked against the authenticated user's permissions and the relevant agency/property scope.

For example, a landlord from Agency A must not be able to access a renter's document belonging to Agency B merely by changing a URL or submitting another tenant's ID.

Do not rely on frontend navigation, hidden buttons, or disabled controls as authorization.



8.4 Protect against common web vulnerabilities
Before release, verify:
- Authentication and session management.
- Multi-factor authentication for privileged users, where appropriate.
- Secure password recovery and account recovery.
- Server-side authorization on every sensitive operation.
- Cross-site scripting and cross-site request forgery defenses
- SQL injection protection through parameterized queries.
- Rate limits on login, code redemption, account lookup, and document access.
- Secure file upload validation and malware scanning.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Audit logging that does not leak sensitive document contents or credentials.
- Backups, restore testing, and incident response procedures.
