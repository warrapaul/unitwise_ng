Tenant App: UI/UX Review

Global issues from the earlier reviews also apply here and are not repeated: contrast, text under 14px, touch targets under 44px, nested cards, placeholders used as values, missing loading/empty/error states, and unsaved-change guards.

A. Global and navigation
Pick one top-level navigation model. The bottom nav (Home, Rent, Payments, Repairs, More) and the drawer (Dashboard, My tenancy, Repairs, Chat…) overlap, and Repairs appears in both.
KEEP THE BOTTOM NAV

Rename or merge “Rent” and “Payments”. Rent shows the statement, Payments shows bills, and the dashboard calls the same data “My rent payments”. Tenants cannot predict which tab has what.

Headers are inconsistent. Chat, Notifications, My tenancy and Edit profile have a back chevron. Rent, Payments, Repairs and Dashboard have none, and their titles sit flush-left at a different offset. Standardize.
Page titles (“Balance & statements”, “My rent payments”) differ from the nav labels (“Rent”, “Payments”). Make them match.
Titles are generic. Show the property or room context (“Room 1-2 · Acacia Court”) in the header.
Add a tenancy switcher for tenants with more than one tenancy or agency.
Remove repeated card titles that echo the page title (“Chat”, “Notifications”, “My rent bills”).
Hide the bottom nav inside forms and conversations (Edit profile, Chat thread, Report an issue).
Terminology is mixed. Landlord, agency, caretaker, agency admin and “the shop” all appear. Tenants do not know “agency”. Use “Property manager” or “Landlord” consistently.
“Tenant profile”, “renter profile” and “renter” are all used. Pick one.
“Unitwise ID” here, “UnitWise ID” elsewhere. Standardize the brand casing.
Money formats vary: “KES 25,000”, “25000”, “23240”, “2240”, “0”. Use “KES 25,000” everywhere.
Phones are shown as +254 700 000 202 on some screens and 254700000202 on others. Use one format everywhere, including inside inputs.
Raw browser controls are unstyled: the Chat search input, the message textarea and the file picker. Use the design-system input component.
Native date and month pickers show a calendar icon and probably mm/dd/yyyy. Use dd/mm/yyyy or a custom picker.
Status colors are inconsistent. “Cancelled” uses error-red, while “In progress”, “Acknowledged” and “Submitted” share the same tan. Use a defined status palette.

Are these details too much to ask? Verdict: yes for most of the last three sections.

The identity and contact blocks are reasonable. The household, work and rental-history sections collect application-grade personal data from people who already live there. Kenya’s Data Protection Act expects data minimisation: collect only what is needed, and only when it is needed.

Field	Verdict
First name, last name, phone	Keep, required
Middle name, email	Keep, optional (mark clearly)
National ID	Keep, but require it only when a landlord requests verification, not to save the profile
Emergency contact (name, phone, relationship)	Keep, optional
ID document upload	Keep, optional, prompted when a landlord requests it
People moving in	Remove for current tenants; ask in an application
Earliest move-in	Remove; meaningless for someone already living there
Anything else (household)	Remove, or rename “Note to landlord” inside an application
Employment status, employer, job title, employed since	Defer to an application; optional
Monthly income	Most sensitive field; make it a range, optional, with “Prefer not to say”; collect only when a landlord asks
Previous landlord name and phone	Defer; this is third-party data and needs a consent note
Previous address, from, to	Defer; optional
Why you left	Remove; free-text blame invites disputes

Recommended structure:

Keep the profile to identity, contact, emergency contact and documents.
Move Household, Work and Rental history into a separate optional “Rental application” that appears only when applying or when a landlord requests it.
Let tenants choose per section what to share, and show it in the share summary.
State why each sensitive field is asked and who can see it.
Hide these sections from tenants who already have an active tenancy unless a landlord asks.
