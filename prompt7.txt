comprehensive UI/UX and product review
Your address feature is doing more than storing postal or delivery addresses. It is establishing a shared address foundation for three different workflows:
1. Property management: landlords create building addresses and associate their properties with Kenya's geographic hierarchy.
2. E-commerce delivery: customers save delivery addresses that buyers, riders and delivery teams can use.
3. Geospatial operations: Super Admin and E-commerce Admin maintain mapped regions, known landmarks and known buildings in QGIS/GIS so riders can locate destinations more reliably.


My main recommendation is to keep the common address hierarchy, introduce a clear distinction between a customer's address and a known delivery destination, and make verification and map coordinates first-class parts of the workflow.

The screenshots already show a good foundation: a progressive geographic registry, hierarchical selection, address defaults, map coordinates, known-building proximity, and rider-only delivery notes. The biggest production-readiness concerns are the relationship between these records, the consistency of the forms, and the meaning of verification.

Important architectural rule: a landlord-created building and an E-commerce Admin-created known delivery building are different business records, even if they happen to describe the same physical building. They can share geographic references and coordinates, but do not automatically merge their identities or permissions.

For example, a landlord may manage WAR1 Apartments in Unitwise. An E-commerce Admin may independently register a known building called WAR1 for deliveries. The customer can associate their address with the known delivery building without giving the e-commerce team access to the landlord's tenancy records.

This separation will help avoid unintended dependencies between property management and e-commerce.

This also makes the design more flexible if a locality spans administrative boundaries.
2.2 Changes to the registry UI
I would retain your horizontal progressive-column design on wide screens, with the following changes.









A. Add a clear record lifecycle

Each record should have an Active/Inactive state. Inactive locations must not be selectable for new addresses, but existing addresses referencing them should remain readable. Deletion should be blocked when records are referenced.












B. Prevent duplicate names

Before creating a county, ward, town or estate, check for a matching name in the relevant geographic context. Warn the administrator and offer the existing record rather than creating a duplicate automatically.












C. Add import and data-quality tools

Administrative geography is tedious to maintain manually. Support bulk import, validation reports, duplicate detection, and a review step before imported records become active.



I would also change the add/edit interaction:
- Use a modal or side panel with the record name, parent reference, status and optional official code.
- Display the full parent path while editing, such as Kiambu County / Githurai Sub-county, so the administrator can confirm the context.
- Warn when changing a parent would affect existing descendants.
- Keep search results, selection and scroll position when a record is edited.
- Show an empty state in each column with a direct action such as “Add the first ward.”
On smaller screens, replace the horizontal columns with a breadcrumb-driven drill-down. Five columns that work at desktop width will otherwise become cramped or require excessive horizontal scrolling.
2.3 Address defaults should be a separate settings screen
The “Set address defaults →” link is a sensible entry point. I would use it for defaults such as:
- Default country: Kenya.
- Default display format for Kenyan physical addresses.
- Default geographic selections, if the application has a genuine business reason to preselect them.
- Optional validation requirements for the address fields.
Do not automatically select a particular county, ward or town merely because it is frequently used. An incorrect default can silently contaminate customer and property data.

5.2 Specific changes to make this production-ready
A. Replace the single ambiguous Location field with an explicit map-location type.

Your form currently says “Location — The gate.” That is useful, but it should represent a defined location type, not merely a free-text label.

Recommended choices:
- Main entrance or gate.
- Customer access point.
- Vehicle drop-off point.
- Building centre, when the entrance is unknown.
A building may eventually need more than one entrance. For the first production version, you can support one primary entrance and add multiple access points later if delivery operations require them.

B. Improve coordinate validation.

When the administrator enters a pin outside a mapped region, display a specific error such as:
This point is outside the mapped delivery regions. Move the pin into a covered region or update the region mapping.

Also show the matched region's name. Do not rely solely on a generic “This area is not covered yet” message.

The server must validate this constraint too. Client-side validation alone is not sufficient.

C. Treat nearby landmarks as suggestions, not automatic associations.

The Ld one (MWI-DIS-001) — 120 m suggestion is good UX, but proximity does not prove that the landmark is relevant.

For each suggestion, let the administrator select it explicitly and optionally record a direction or relationship:
- Near the main entrance.
- Opposite the building.
- Behind the building.
- Along the access road.
Keep the distance visible. If there are several landmarks, allow multiple associations rather than only one.

D. Add duplicate detection.

Before saving a known building, check for likely duplicates using its name, code, mapped location and nearby address context. Warn about potential matches without automatically merging records.

E. Improve the status lifecycle.

An Active checkbox is enough for basic enablement, but a production system should distinguish:
- Draft — being created or checked.
- Active — usable for address association.
- Inactive — retained for history but not offered for new matches.
- Needs review — location or building information is disputed or unreliable.
You can implement the extra statuses only where the operational workflow needs them; the critical requirement is that a record cannot silently become a trusted delivery destination just because someone created it.

6.2 Specific changes to the rider workflow
1. Do not make riders re-enter the entire address unnecessarily.

The rider should see the current address in a compact, readable format. The normal task is to correct a specific field, associate a known building, add a unit number, or record access directions.

2. Make the association with a known building explicit.

Add a separate section:
- Known delivery building — search and select.
- Matched building address and code.
- Distance between the customer pin and the building entrance.
- Whether the selected building belongs to a mapped region.
- Action to unlink or change the association, subject to permissions.
Do not overwrite the customer's address merely because a nearby known building was selected. Keep both the customer's submitted address and the canonical delivery destination.

3. Separate verification from editing.

The current “I have been there — mark this address verified” wording is a good starting point, but it conflates two separate claims:
- The rider visited and checked the destination.
- The customer has confirmed that the address is correct.
Use explicit statuses:
- Unverified
- Field verified
- Customer confirmation pending
- Customer confirmed
- Needs review
A rider's field verification must not automatically imply customer confirmation. Also record who verified it and when.

4. Add a confirmation request workflow.

When the rider changes important customer-facing details, such as the phone, unit number or main destination, the system should be able to request customer confirmation. This need not block every delivery, but the address should remain marked as pending confirmation until the customer responds.

5. Add audit history.

Show who changed the address, what changed and when. Preserve the previous values, particularly for phone numbers, coordinates, customer-visible directions and known-building associations.

This matters when a subsequent rider cannot locate the customer and needs to understand whether the problem arose from the original address or a later edit.

7. The most important integration: associate customer addresses with known delivery buildings
I would make this the central feature connecting the address form, GIS registry and rider workflow.

A customer can have a perfectly valid delivery address without having an association with a known building. Conversely, a known building may exist before any customer has registered an address there.

The UI must support both situations.
Recommended association states

1. No match yet

Customer address exists, but no known delivery building is linked. The rider can search or continue using the customer's directions and pin.




2. Match suggested

The system finds nearby buildings using coordinates, names and geographic context. It presents candidates with distances and identifiers, but does not link them automatically.




3. Association confirmed

An authorised user selects the correct known building. The address retains its own unit details and delivery notes.




4. Association disputed or outdated

The customer or rider reports that the linked destination is incorrect. The association can be reviewed without deleting the customer address or the canonical building.



How matching should work
Use a combination of:
- Exact known-building code, when available.
- Coordinate proximity.
- Normalized building name.
- County, locality and estate/area context.
- Optional landmark associations.
Do not treat proximity alone as a definitive match. A customer pin may be at a gate, a nearby shop or a neighbouring apartment building.

For the first release, matching can be a suggestion list. You do not need an elaborate automatic matching engine to make this workflow useful.

8. Data model and ownership rules that the UI must enforce
The forms will only behave consistently if the backend distinguishes these records. I would establish the following logical model before adding more address-related features.
Record	Owner / authority	Purpose
County, sub-county, ward	Super Admin	Shared administrative geography
Town/locality, estate/area, street	Address registry administrators	Shared physical place references
Landmark	E-commerce/GIS administration	Navigation reference
Geo building	E-commerce/GIS administration	Canonical delivery destination
Property building	Landlord/agency	Managed property and tenancy records
Customer address	Individual customer	Saved delivery destination and contact details
Address-to-geo-building association	Authorised delivery workflow	Links a customer address to a known destination
Rider observations	Delivery operations	Internal access details and field observations
Verification history	Relevant authorised actors	Who confirmed what, when and how

These are logical boundaries; they do not necessarily require separate database tables for every item if your existing architecture uses embedded value objects or related entities.
Important constraints
1. A customer address must survive a geo-building deactivation. Keep its submitted address, historical association and delivery history. A deactivated destination should not be offered for new associations.
2. A property building must not be deleted because a GIS record is removed. The two records have different ownership and business purposes.
3. A known building can have many customer addresses. Apartment residents can share one building while having different unit numbers, phone numbers and access instructions.
4. An address should retain its own coordinates. A building entrance coordinate is not necessarily the customer's precise unit or delivery point.
5. Verification must be attributable. Record the actor, timestamp, verification type and relevant evidence or outcome.
6. Customer-facing and internal notes must have separate permissions. Rider-only access information should not automatically appear in customer address screens or shared order documents.
7. Geographic references should be stable. Renaming an estate should update its displayed name without breaking historical orders or property records.
These rules prevent the shared address system from becoming tightly coupled to either property management or e-commerce.
9. Validation and error states to add before production
The screenshots focus on successful form states. I would explicitly design the following failure and recovery states.
Situation	Expected UI behaviour
County changed	Clear incompatible descendant selections and require revalidation of the remaining location fields.
Sub-county changed	Revalidate the ward; do not retain an invalid ward silently.
Locality or estate unavailable	Allow permitted free-text entry and distinguish it from a registry-backed selection.
Invalid map link	Show a useful error and preserve the original input.
Latitude/longitude outside valid ranges	Reject the coordinates before saving.
Pin outside a covered region	Identify the region constraint and allow the administrator to correct the pin or mapping.
Potential duplicate known building	Display the existing candidate and offer review before saving a duplicate.
Known building deactivated	Keep historical associations readable; prompt users to select an active replacement where necessary.
Rider edits a customer address	Save an audit entry and apply the correct customer-confirmation status.
Customer changes default address	Update the default atomically and handle concurrent changes safely.
Save fails	Preserve entered values and allow retry without re-entering the form.
Location permission denied	Allow manual pin placement, pasted coordinates or text directions.

For the address registry, also handle records that are merged, renamed or deactivated. Never silently reassign existing addresses to another ward or building just because a name looks similar.

11. Recommended implementation order
I would not implement every enhancement simultaneously. The following sequence establishes the foundations first.

Phase 1 — Correct the shared address foundation
Highest priority
- Finalize the administrative versus physical geography model.
- Define required and optional fields for each workflow.
- Add active/inactive and safe deactivation rules.
- Standardize the address display format and validation.
- Define ownership and permissions for customer, property and GIS records.




Phase 2 — Improve address entry and map accuracy
- Improve customer form grouping and coordinate entry.
- Add map preview, entrance pin selection and coordinate validation.
- Improve the landlord building address editor.
- Add duplicate detection and registry quality checks.




Phase 3 — Connect GIS destinations to customer addresses
- Implement search and suggestions for known buildings.
- Add explicit association and unlink/reassignment actions.
- Preserve customer-specific unit and access information.
- Enforce mapped-region and active-destination rules.




Phase 4 — Verification and operational quality
- Introduce field verification and customer confirmation as separate states.
- Add address-change audit history.
- Provide internal quality indicators for known buildings.
- Measure failed deliveries caused by inaccurate addresses and improve matching from those outcomes.



12. How to know the feature is production-ready
I would consider the address feature ready when these end-to-end scenarios work reliably:
- A landlord creates a building using shared geographic references, then creates rooms and tenancy records without address changes affecting those records.
- A customer saves an address without knowing their coordinates and can still complete checkout.
- A customer pastes a valid map link and can verify that the resulting pin is correct.
- A rider finds an existing known building, links the customer's address to it, and records the correct unit and access instructions.
- A rider corrects an address without accidentally claiming that the customer confirmed it.
- A known building is deactivated without breaking existing customer addresses or historical orders.
- A Super Admin renames or deactivates a geographic record without corrupting dependent data.
- Two customers living in the same apartment building retain separate addresses and delivery instructions.
- A building outside the delivery coverage area cannot be accidentally activated as a valid destination.
- A failed save or interrupted map interaction does not destroy the user's entered information.
I would also track the percentage of completed deliveries with verified destination coordinates, the rate of successful known-building matches, the frequency of address corrections, and failed deliveries attributed to location errors.
My final recommendation
The design does not need a complete visual overhaul. Its existing visual language is consistent, the progressive registry is understandable, and the customer/rider forms already capture most of the required information.

The most important changes are structural and workflow-specific:
1. Correct the geographic hierarchy and keep administrative geography separate from physical place names.
2. Distinguish landlord-managed property buildings from GIS-managed delivery buildings.
3. Make the map pin and building entrance meaningful rather than treating coordinates as just another pair of fields.
4. Make known-building association a core part of the rider workflow.
5. Separate field verification, customer confirmation and internal delivery notes.
6. Preserve history and enforce record ownership, validation and permissions across the whole feature.
If you implement only one major enhancement after the shared address foundation, I would choose the known-building association workflow, because it connects your GIS investment directly to a practical improvement in delivery operations without requiring customers to learn a more complicated address form.
