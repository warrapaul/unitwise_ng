Landing Page: UI/UX and Conversion Review

The copy is strong. The headline, the before/after framing and the “we never hold your money” message are all good. The page tells landlords what the product does but never shows it, never states a price, and offers no proof that anyone uses it. Those three gaps will cost the most sign-ups.

I reviewed downscaled screenshots, so text sizes below are approximate. Verify them at 100% zoom.

A. Critical gaps (fix first)
There is not one product screenshot on the page. The copy says “every item is a working screen,” yet it shows stock photos. Add real dashboard, billing, tenant-app and M-Pesa reconciliation screens.
There is no pricing section. “What does it cost?” is the last collapsed FAQ item. Show your plans (Starter, Growth, Scale) with the room-based pricing and the free trial terms.
There is no social proof: no testimonials, landlord logos, portfolio size, KES processed or case study. Landlords will not hand over rent collection to an unknown brand without it.
The only real-looking number is “KES 1,240,000” in the hero card, and it is sample data. Replace it with real figures, or remove it.
There is no way to talk to a human. Add a WhatsApp chat button, a phone number, and a “Book a demo” option. Kenyan landlords buy on trust and conversation.
Trust and security are not addressed: data protection, who sees tenant data, backups, Data Protection Act compliance, and how M-Pesa credentials are stored.
The trial terms are unclear. “Get started free”, “Start free trial” and “Start your free trial” are three labels for one action. Say whether it is free forever or a trial, how long, how many rooms, and whether a card or PayBill is needed.
A product video or 60-second walkthrough is missing.
B. Header and navigation
The header has only Sign in and Get started free. Add anchor links: How it works, Features, Pricing, FAQ, Contact.
Make the header sticky, with the CTA always visible.
Sign in and Get started free are small, and Sign in is low contrast. Increase the button height to at least 44px.
The logo is a small “U” tile plus the wordmark. Make the brand more distinct.
C. Hero
The headline is good. Keep it, but increase its size and give it more contrast.
The sub-headline is a four-clause sentence in small grey text. Cut it to two lines.
“Takes rent straight into your own PayBill” conflicts with “we never hold your money” unless read carefully. Reword to “Rent goes straight to your PayBill or till.”
The sub-headline column is narrower than the headline, which looks accidental. Align the widths.
The eyebrow tag “For landlords & letting agents in Kenya” is about 9px and low contrast. Make it readable.
The two CTAs are small and similar. Make the primary larger, and make “See how a month runs” a text link or a play-video button.
The three trust ticks are tiny and low contrast, and they wrap awkwardly. Put them directly under the CTA as one clean row, with icons.
“Works on any phone” is unclear. Does it mean no app install, SMS fallback, or feature phones? Say which.
The hero image is a staged house model with keys. It is a generic real-estate cliché and not Kenyan. Replace it with a real product mockup on a phone and laptop, or a local apartment-block photo.
The “Collections · this month” card is labelled “this month” but shows six months (Apr–Sep). Fix the label.
The chart has no axis values, and “96% on time · Sample data” is tiny. Replace it with a real screenshot.
The card overlaps the image edge in a way that looks accidental. Anchor it properly.
Show the product proof above the fold, not only a photo.
D. Stats strip
“1 click”, “0 shillings”, “24/7” and “Every building” are claims, not proof. “0 shillings” is confusing at first read.
Replace them with real metrics: landlords onboarded, rooms managed, KES collected, average setup time.
Each stat’s description is tiny grey text. Increase it.
Add a divider or card treatment so the strip reads as its own section.
“1 click bills every room” overpromises. The product’s billing flow has several steps (generate, readings, review, payments, balances). Say “Bill every room in minutes.”
E. “Rent day, before and after” table
The “Today” and “With Unitwise” columns look the same. Style them differently, for example muted red or grey for Today and green with check icons for Unitwise.
The row-label column is wide and mostly empty, while the content columns are cramped. Rebalance the widths.
Body text is about 11px. Raise it to 16px.
There are no icons per row. Add one to each of the five rows for scanning.
The section header sits directly against the stats strip with almost no spacing. Add vertical space.
Each “Today” paragraph is long. Cut it to one line.
Show a visual outcome per row, such as a mini screenshot or a chip.
F. “From bills out to money in, in four steps”
The four photos are unrelated to the steps. Step 2 shows a phone with social-media icons (not M-Pesa), and steps 3 and 4 show living rooms. Use product screens.
Photos are tall and push the text down. Use screenshots with short captions.
Step text is about 11px. Increase it.
Cards look identical, with no progress line or arrows connecting steps. Add a visual flow.
The heading collides with the table above it. Add spacing.
Add a link per step to a short demo.
G. “Tenants who can see their balance” section
The left column is mostly empty, and the right column is seven stacked boxes. Show the tenant app in a phone frame on the left.
Seven items is too many. Group them into three: Pay, Track, Communicate.
The claim “Half of the calls a landlord takes…” has no source. Back it up or soften it.
Item titles and descriptions are close in size and weight. Strengthen the hierarchy.
The green-grey band background lowers text contrast. Verify AA contrast.
H. “One system instead of…” feature grid
Six dense columns of bullets in about 11px text are a wall of text. Reduce each group to three bullets with icons, and link “See all features.”
Card heights are uneven (Tenants and leases is taller).
The headline “notebook, three spreadsheets and a WhatsApp group” is good. Keep it, and move this section after pricing and proof.
“Not a roadmap. Every item below is a working screen” is a strong claim. Verify every bullet before launch (see section N).
Add a screenshot or hover preview for each group.
Bullets do not distinguish core features from extras. Mark the top three.
I. “Everyone sees their part” (roles)
The avatar photos are tiny stock faces that imply real people. Use illustrations or icons.
The cards are large and carry little information. Compress them into one row of four with role icons.
Show a mini-diagram of what each role can and cannot see, since this is a real differentiator.
Mention the permission model explicitly (agency-wide, building-level, caretaker).
J. “Running by the end of the afternoon”
The steps have no visuals and no time estimates. Add “5 min” or “10 min” to each.
The step text is tiny, and the next heading (“Before you sign up”) sits directly against it. Add spacing.
“Running by the end of the afternoon” is a promise. Support it with what you do to help: data import, onboarding call, WhatsApp help.
Add a CTA at the end of this section.
K. FAQ
Every answer is collapsed, so nobody can scan them and search engines may not index them. Open the top three by default.
The FAQ occupies half the width and leaves the right half empty. Use a two-column layout or centre it.
Move “What does it cost?” to the top.
Add the missing objections:
Is my data secure, and who can see it?
Can I import from Excel?
What do tenants without smartphones do?
Which PayBill, till and bank options are supported?
What are the M-Pesa fees, and who pays them?
What happens if I cancel?
Is there a contract or lock-in?
What support do you offer, and when?
Does it work for landlords with only one or two buildings?
Make the arrows and rows tappable at 44px.
Add a “Still unsure? Chat on WhatsApp” prompt below.
L. Final CTA and footer
The dark banner image is murky, and the text sits on a low-contrast background. Use a solid colour or a lighter image.
“Sign in” in the final banner competes with the sign-up CTA. Remove it.
“Start your free trial” repeats the CTA, which is fine. Add the trial terms beneath it.
The footer has only the logo, a tagline and “Photography: Unsplash.” Add:
Contact (phone, WhatsApp, email)
Terms and Privacy policy
Support and Help centre
Pricing and Features links
Company name and registration
Social links
Move the photography credit to a smaller line or an About page.
M. Visual design and typography
Body text across the page is about 11–12px. Set a minimum of 16px for body and 14px for captions.
Eyebrow labels (WHY LANDLORDS SWITCH, HOW A MONTH RUNS…) are tiny uppercase grey. Increase their size and contrast.
Section headings are all the same size and weight, so there is no rhythm. Vary scale between sections.
Vertical spacing between sections is inconsistent. Several headings sit directly against the previous section’s content.
The palette is almost entirely muted green and grey, so CTAs do not stand out. Add one strong accent for primary actions.
Cards have faint borders on a light background and look flat. Strengthen elevation or use solid fills.
Photography has no consistent style: a house model, a phone, interiors and headshots. Use one visual language, preferably real product UI.
Buttons are about 28–32px tall in the capture. Make them at least 44px.
Large empty areas appear in the tenant section, the FAQ and the hero text column. Balance each layout.
The layout is a long single column with little interactivity. Add tabs, a carousel for screenshots, and short animations that show the flow, with reduced-motion support.
N. Claims versus the product

Check each claim against what the product does today.

“Bill every room in 1 click.” The building page shows a multi-step cycle with meter readings, review and confirm.
“Select the late ones and send a reminder to all of them at once.” The tenant list has no multi-select or reminder action.
“Notices and reminders by SMS, WhatsApp, email or app notification.” Confirm that SMS and WhatsApp exist.
“Deposits… refunded or kept.” The tenant screens show only recording a deposit.
“Each payment is matched to their room and month automatically.” Make sure the Daraja integration is live and not sandbox.
“Tenants get an M-Pesa prompt on their phone.” Confirm this is production.
“Half of the calls a landlord takes…” Needs evidence.
“Everything below is a working screen.” Every bullet must be true on launch day.
Brand casing is “Unitwise” here and “UnitWise” in the app. Pick one.
The landing page says landlord, caretaker and tenant, while the app says agency, admin and renter. Use the landing page’s terms in the product.
O. Mobile
The page is extremely long. Collapse sections: tabs for before/after, a carousel for the four steps, and accordions for the feature groups.
The before/after table becomes a long stack of small paragraphs. Use a swipeable comparison or one toggle (“Today / With Unitwise”).
Four tall photos stack in the “four steps” section and take several screens. Use a horizontal swipe with small images.
Add a sticky bottom CTA bar (“Start free trial” plus a WhatsApp icon).
Make the hero image and chart card smaller, and place the CTA before the image.
Stats are in a 2×2 grid with tiny captions. Increase the text size.
Body text is small and lines are cramped. Increase the font size and line height.
The final banner image is barely visible on mobile. Remove it or lighten it.
The footer wraps awkwardly, with “Photography: Unsplash” on its own line. Restructure it with links.
P. Performance, SEO, accessibility and legal

Performance

Many large stock images load. Serve WebP or AVIF, lazy-load below the fold, set image dimensions, and target under 1 MB of page weight.
Prevent layout shift (the hero card and image areas).

SEO

Use one H1 and a logical heading order.
Write a title and meta description with local keywords (“rent collection software Kenya”, “property management software Nairobi”).
Add Open Graph tags and an image for WhatsApp link previews, since the link will be shared on WhatsApp.
Add alt text, FAQ schema and a sitemap.
Add pages for pricing, features and contact for indexing.

Accessibility

Fix text contrast on grey and green backgrounds (WCAG AA 4.5:1).
Add visible focus states and a skip link.
Make FAQ items keyboard operable, using details/summary or proper ARIA.
Add alt text to all images and make decorative ones empty.
Do not rely on colour alone to separate “Today” and “With Unitwise.”

Legal and trust

Add Terms, Privacy and cookie notice links.
State Data Protection Act compliance and data registration.
Show a company name, address and support contact.
Credit M-Pesa and Safaricom correctly and use approved logos only with permission.
Q. Recommended page order
Hero with a real product screenshot, the primary CTA, trial terms and trust ticks.
Real proof: numbers, testimonials, logos.
Before/after (visual).
Product walkthrough with screenshots and a short video.
How a month runs (four steps, with product screens).
Tenant benefits (with phone mockup).
Key features (condensed to six cards).
Roles and permissions (compact).
Pricing (Starter, Growth, Scale, plus trial).
Getting started (with time estimates and onboarding help).
FAQ (cost first).
Final CTA and a complete footer.
